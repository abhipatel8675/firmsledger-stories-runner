## About
Harpak-ULMA Packaging, LLC is headquartered in Taunton, Massachusetts. The business traces its roots to 1994 when the Harlfinger family, drawing on over 30 years of packaging experience, established Harpak. Primary markets include food processing (bakery, poultry, cheese, snack, and case-ready packaging), medical device packaging, and industrial packaging. The firm works with corporations in those sectors and, since 2024, supports Cabinplant weighing and packaging equipment owners in the U.S. and Canada through a full partnership.

Harpak-ULMA cites its six core values—integrity, commitment, service, excellence, teamwork, and innovation—as the foundation of its differentiation. ## About
Hawaii Medical Research Center operates a cadaver laboratory in Honolulu, Hawaii, established to provide a dedicated venue for medical education, research, and surgical skill development. The facility was created in response to growing societal concern for medical safety and rapid advances in medical technology, which have increased demand for hands-on training that cannot be conducted on living patients.

The center provides a fully equipped cadaver lab where physicians, dentists, and other healthcare professionals can practice advanced surgical techniques, test new medical devices, and conduct anatomical research. Cadavers are sourced through the American Association of Tissue Banks, with each donor screened for infectious disease and accompanied by safety documentation. The facility operates under Hawaii state licensure and follows the Uniform Anatomical Gift Act (1968), which governs anatomical donation in the United States.

Primary users include orthopedic surgeons, neurosurgeons, plastic surgeons, dental specialists, and medical researchers from Japan and other countries. The center has supported research projects funded by Japan's Grants-in-Aid for Scientific Research (Kakenhi) and has exhibited at major Japanese medical society meetings, including the Japanese Orthopaedic Association, Japan Neurosurgical Society, and Japanese Society of Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery. It also collaborates with Kauai Search & Rescue, providing cadavers for K-9 search dog training.

The center was founded to address the lack of accessible cadaver-based training environments in Japan, where legal restrictions limit anatomical dissection to specific university settings. By operating in Hawaii under U.S. law, it offers a legally compliant alternative for international medical professionals seeking realistic surgical practice and device validation.
