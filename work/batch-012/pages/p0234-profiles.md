# Revolution Bag

**Old description:** Delta Plastics of the South is founded in Stuttgart, Arkansas, with a focus on providing efficient irrigation solutions to farmers in the Mid-South, and soon after starts recycling to show the importance of being part of the solution to plastic waste.

## About
Revolution Bag, operating as Revolution Company, traces its roots to 1996 when Delta Plastics of the South was founded in Stuttgart, Arkansas. The company began by supplying efficient irrigation solutions to farmers in the Mid-South region and quickly added recycling operations to address plastic waste. In 2008 the business expanded to Little Rock, where it established its first sustainable-loop recycling facility and launched the Revolution Bag line of trash can liners made with up to 97% certified post-consumer resin. The headquarters remain in Little Rock, Arkansas.

Today Revolution manufactures a range of plastic products including trash can liners, carryout bags for foodservice and retail, agricultural films, commercial sheeting, and stretch films. The company runs an integrated circular model: a specialized fleet collects used film, which is cleaned and mechanically recycled at its facilities to produce certified post-consumer and post-industrial resins. Those resins feed both Revolution’s own product lines and the supply chains of global brands and OEMs. The company reports processing more than 300 million pounds of material annually and has diverted over 3 billion pounds from landfills since inception. Growth has come through acquisitions such as Rodeo Plastic Bag & Film, Command Packaging, Encore Recycling, Pak-Sher, Polar Plastics, Jadcore, and Five Point Films, adding capacity in commercial films, consumer bags, post-industrial recycling, and stretch films.

Revolution serves agriculture, food service, construction and commercial supply, consumer goods, restaurants, delis, and grocery chains. Its recycled resins are sold to brands and original equipment manufacturers seeking circular content. A noted reference comes from Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport, where a sustainability manager reported reducing virgin resin use from hundreds of thousands of pounds to 17,000 pounds annually through Revolution’s programs.

The company was started to demonstrate that plastic can be a valuable resource with infinite usefulness if reclaimed responsibly. Its stated differentiator is a closed-loop business model that controls the entire lifecycle — from collection and recycling to resin production and finished goods — enabling customers to meet ESG goals while maintaining product performance.
