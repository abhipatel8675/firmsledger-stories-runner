## About
Music City Solar operates from Madison, Tennessee, as a program of Nashville Electric Service (NES), a public utility. The initiative launched on August 1, 2018, when the utility transformed a former landfill off I-65 into Nashville's first community solar park. The project began after customers expressed demand for renewable energy options, prompting NES to develop a shared solar facility that would allow broad participation without rooftop installations.

The program manages a 17,020-panel solar array that feeds clean energy directly into the NES grid. Customers purchase subscriptions to individual panels and receive a monthly solar credit on their electric bills through 2038. As of the latest data, 3,142 panels are leased to customers, generating over 21 million kilowatt-hours to date. The park also reserves panels for the Solar Angel Fund, a donation-based component that provides energy assistance to low-income households; 1,015 panels have been donated so far, with 12,863 panels still available for new subscribers.

The service targets residential customers — both homeowners and renters — across the NES service territory who want renewable energy without upfront installation costs or maintenance. It also serves low-income households through the Solar Angel donation channel, administered in partnership with The Community Foundation of Middle Tennessee. Commercial or industrial participation is not described in the available materials.

NES frames the program as a direct response to customer voice: "The city spoke. We listened." The utility emphasizes maintenance-free participation, long-term bill credits, and a community-focused model that combines environmental impact with local energy assistance. The park's location on a reclaimed landfill underscores a dual benefit of land reuse and emissions reduction.
