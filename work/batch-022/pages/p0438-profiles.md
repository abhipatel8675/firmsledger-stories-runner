## About
Southrock Engineering Inc. is based in Mesa, Arizona, and was founded in 2006 by Peter and Christine Hall. Both had worked in the electronics industry for over 20 years, and in 2006 they acquired JR Electronics from the estate of its original founder, a company that had produced specialized cable assemblies for industrial customers for more than a decade. With backgrounds in electronic hardware design, microprocessors, and software, the Halls expanded from contract design projects into full electronic manufacturing, investing in modern wire processing, crimping equipment, and surface-mount pick-and-place capability.

The company designs and assembles custom printed circuit board assemblies (PCBAs) using both surface-mount and through-hole processes, builds custom cable and wire harness assemblies, provides full-service box build assembly with enclosure integration, and offers PCB and cable assembly prototyping from single units to small batches. It also performs PCBA reverse engineering to address obsolete designs, hard-to-source components, or offshore vendor constraints. All assembly work is performed to IPC standards; the shop is UL certified and RoHS compliant. There are no minimum order quantities, and the facility supports volumes from prototype through production runs.

Southrock serves a range of sectors including aerospace and defense, commercial, industrial, marine, telecommunications, automotive, energy, lighting, health, safety, and utility markets. Customers include manufacturers of automation and control systems, monitoring systems, LED and lighting solutions, electronics and power solutions, and specialized automation equipment.

The business was started to provide a complete range of electronic design and assembly under one roof, and its reverse engineering service was developed specifically to help clients re-engineer PCBA and wire harness products to avoid tariffs and overcome supply-chain hurdles with offshore vendors. The company emphasizes local engineering support, quick turnaround, and freight savings from its Mesa facility.
