## About
Tucson Custom Sheet Metal operates from a shop in Tucson, Arizona. The company has served the Tucson area for over 30 years, establishing itself as a local source for sheet metal roofing components. Ownership details and founder names are not publicly disclosed.

The shop produces both stock and custom sheet metal roofing products, including flashing, scuppers, vents, cooler jacks, caps, and pipe jacks. Core processes include cutting, bending, and soldering; scupper and jack joints are fully soldered for water-tight seals. The facility stocks material in gauges ranging from 28GA to 20GA, with 20GA representing the maximum thickness the equipment can cut and bend. Primary customers are roofing contractors and construction projects throughout the Tucson region. The company maintains a stock inventory of standard flashing, scuppers, and vents for immediate pickup or quick shipment, while also fabricating custom pieces for unique project requirements.

The business emphasizes durability, noting that its products are made to last with fully soldered, water-tight joints. It positions itself as a local supplier capable of fulfilling both standard orders from stock and custom fabrications with short lead times for the Tucson market.

## About
Two Sisters Mill & Mercantile operates in Jeffersonville, Vermont, at The Granary on Route 15. The business was founded by sisters Monica Case and Tamra Higgins, who trace their Vermont ancestry to Revolutionary War veterans Josiah Pond and a Sheldon settler. Tamra’s purchase of twin Shetland sheep over a decade ago sparked the venture into fiber, while Monica brought 34 years of healthcare administration and occupational therapy experience. The shop reflects a family-owned, small-business ethos rooted in the region’s agricultural heritage.

The shop functions as a retail mercantile and community hub, stocking yarn, raw fiber, and Schacht spinning and weaving equipment—both in-store and by special order. Inventory rotates through goods from more than 60 consignors, and the sisters host free weekly fiber hours and monthly spinning sessions. No manufacturing occurs on site; the operation centers on curation, education, and direct sales.

Customers include hand-spinners, knitters, crocheters, felters, and fiber tourists visiting the area. The shop serves local artisans seeking supplies and a venue for their work, as well as travelers drawn by Vermont’s fiber trail. Tamra’s own flock of Shetland and Shetland/Finn sheep supplies some of the raw material sold in the store.

The founders frame the business as a way to honor the alchemy of turning land and animal fiber into crafted goods, aiming to build a fair, sustainable marketplace for livestock raisers and fiber artists. This mission drives their emphasis on in-person shopping, consignment partnerships, and community events such as a planned 2027 fiber tour of Scotland.
