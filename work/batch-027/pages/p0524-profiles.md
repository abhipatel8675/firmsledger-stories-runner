ECM USA operates from Pleasant Prairie, Wisconsin, positioned between Milwaukee and Chicago. The company designs and manufactures vacuum furnace systems using cold-wall, electric heating technology that eliminates open flames and enables higher temperature treatments with easy integration into machining lines. Its furnace portfolio includes the ECO double-chamber, JUMBO high-volume, FLEX modular, NANO small-batch, VESUVE nitriding/FNC, FULGURA multi-flow, TURQUOISE horizontal, LILLIPUT hearth-lift, CRISTAL bell, and PFTH vertical oil-quench models, with maximum work zones up to 1,500 mm and load capacities to 2,000 kg. Core processes cover low-pressure vacuum carburizing (LPC), carbonitriding, brazing, sintering, vacuum induction melting, vacuum arc remelting, tempering, vacuum purged gas nitriding, ferritic nitrocarburizing, and vapor-phase aluminizing. Through the ECM Greentech division, it supplies semiconductor wafer-processing furnaces for diffusion, oxidation, doping, and rapid thermal processing up to 300 mm wafers, plus silicon ingot casting and crystal growth equipment. ECM Robotics provides automation integration ranging from cleanroom wafer handling to heavy automotive loads exceeding 1,000 kg.

ECM USA serves the automotive, aerospace, marine, agricultural, semiconductor, solar/photovoltaic, and general industrial heat-treat sectors, as well as university and R&D laboratories. Field service, installation, training, and aftermarket parts availability are positioned as integral to the offering, supported by the ECM Group's 50-plus years of engineering and design experience.

After several ownership changes and relocations — through Costa Mesa, Irvine, and Santa Ana, California — the management team bought out all shareholders in late 1990, financed the move to New Mexico, and reincorporated the business there.

The company designs and manufactures a broad line of transformers and related components. Its catalog includes audio matching transformers (microphone, line level, combiners, splitters), audio distribution transformers, stereo-to-mono couplers, classic tube audio transformers (single-ended, push-pull, interstage), power transformers for tube and solid-state equipment, chokes and inductors (DC power chokes, high-voltage plate/filament), and a range of parts such as coil bobbins (nylon 6/6 glass-filled), steel laminations, end bells, brackets, and mounting hardware. EDCOR also offers custom transformer design and sells amplifier replacement transformers and kit transformers for brands such as Fender, Dynaco, and Decware.

EDCOR serves professional audio installers, musicians, amplifier builders, and hobbyists. Its distribution transformers were used in the refit of the Rose Bowl in the mid-to-late 1980s. Current customers include tube amplifier designers like Pete Millett and Bruce Heran (OddWatt), kit builders (Decware ZEN kits), and repair shops needing replacements for vintage Fender, Dynaco, and other amplifiers. The company also supports the DIY community with technical blogs and design resources.

EDCOR emphasizes domestic manufacturing: "Proudly designed and built in 'MERICA," with a stated effort to source materials and parts from U.S. suppliers. The transformer production process has evolved over four decades, originally driven by the company's own difficulty in obtaining quality audio and power transformers in the early 1980s, which led to the acquisition of Delta Magnetics and its manufacturing facilities.
