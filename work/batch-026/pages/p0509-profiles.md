## About
Located in Charleston, South Carolina, the Charleston Woodworking School was founded by Master Craftsman Sam Sprouse, a graduate of the Thomas Chippendale School of Furniture in Scotland. The school operates from a state-of-the-art facility and is licensed by the South Carolina Commission on Higher Education.

The school delivers a nine-month professional woodworking program that emphasizes traditional techniques. Curriculum covers furniture design, furniture making, antique restoration and repair, specialty wood projects, project management, and marketing. Instruction combines classroom theory with hands-on shop work; each student designs and builds a major furniture piece per semester. Enrollment is capped at 15 students per academic year to ensure one-on-one tutoring and a self-paced learning environment. The shop remains open after hours for independent project development.

The program serves individuals seeking a career in woodworking, including veterans who can use VA benefits. Graduates are prepared to pursue furniture design, custom furniture making, antique restoration, or to launch their own woodworking businesses. The school also teaches marketing strategies aimed at interior designers, home builders, antique dealers, and private clients.

Differentiators cited by the school include its status as the only professional woodworking school in South Carolina, VA-approved benefits, small class sizes, a faculty of local woodworking experts and business professionals, and a sustainability focus that prioritizes locally harvested lumber.

## About
City Bench operates from a shop in Higganum, Connecticut, with a milling facility in nearby Hamden. The company was founded by Ted Esselstyn and Zeb Esselstyn as a limited liability company. The idea emerged when Ted, an artist and furniture maker, was working on a Connecticut Trash Museum exhibit about throw-away culture and recognized that street trees removed by municipalities could be upcycled into meaningful furniture.

The company transforms urban trees—salvaged from cities, towns, and institutions—into one-of-a-kind furniture, slabs, and lumber. Workflows include design, milling, fabrication, and final assembly, all performed by hand at their Connecticut shops. The team includes shop manager Ben Komola and sculptor Mike Donovan, though total headcount is not disclosed.

City Bench serves education and nonprofit clients such as Yale University, Harvard University, and the University of Connecticut; commercial customers including PepsiCo, Bridgewater Associates, and lululemon; museums like the Florence Griswold Museum and Mystic Seaport Museum; and healthcare institutions such as Yale New Haven Hospital and Connecticut Children’s Medical Center. The company also works directly with municipalities, arborists, architects, and interior designers.

Each piece ships with a “birth certificate” documenting the tree’s origin and story, reflecting the founders’ goal to create positive environmental and social change by turning a waste stream into a value stream.
