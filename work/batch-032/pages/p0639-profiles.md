## About
Sonoran Stitch Factory operates from Tucson, Arizona. The company was founded by Erica Yngve, a serial entrepreneur who also owns three brands manufactured at the facility: Bralessly, Postcraft Products, and Palmfree Sunwear. Yngve’s background includes manufacturing operations, an import partnership, and an MBA in operations management from DePaul University.

The factory specializes in sewn goods production with a focus on knit apparel and small to medium batch runs. Its services span production flow management, inventory control, fulfillment, shipping, and quality assurance. The team reports 27-plus client brands and more than 160,000 units sewn to date, supported by a skilled operator base and a production manager with deep textile experience.

Customers range from emerging apparel labels to established industrial suppliers. Postcraft Products, a 90-year-old company, relies on the factory for industrial sewn goods used in housing, healthcare, hospitality, and cruise ships. Palmfree Sunwear produces sun-protective gloves for golf, tennis, pickleball, and gardening. The factory also partners with brands like Bralessly in the fashion space.

The company’s stated mission centers on three pillars: building a sustainable, profitable, human-centered production model; delivering high-quality domestic manufacturing; and creating pathways into manufacturing careers through apprenticeships and student training (209 students trained, two apprentices sponsored). ## About
South Fork Industries operates in Maiden, North Carolina, where it began production in 1988 as a commission dyeing and finishing operation. The company has run continuously for over three decades, building a reputation for quality work and integrity in the textile processing sector.

The facility houses 14 dye machines, including pressure jets and soft-flow units, with load capacities ranging from 150 to 1,700 pounds. A centralized computer system controls the dye machines and links to an automated dye and chemical mixing room for precise adjustments. Finishing capabilities cover both tubular and open-width formats: two shrinkage-control belt dryers, 24 tumble dryers, four Fabcon compactors, and tubular napping for tubular goods; a Krantz eight-bay tenter frame with a 74-inch maximum width, a Fabcon open-width compactor, open-width napping, and tip shearing for open-width fabrics.

South Fork serves textile mills, apparel brands, and fabric converters that require commission dyeing and finishing, though the company does not publicly list specific customers or industry verticals beyond the general textile market.

The company differentiates itself through an on-site laboratory that specializes in color matching using Datacolor Color Management Solutions, and it conducts a full suite of physical tests—shrinkage, width, yield, stretch and recovery, torque, crocking, wash fastness, burst, and wicking—all performed to AATCC test methods.
