## About
Grace Technologies is headquartered in Davenport, Iowa. The company traces its roots to 1993 when founder Phil Allen, then a startup system integrator called Grace Engineering, encountered a hazardous electrical safety issue during a friction welder retrofit at John Deere. The solution he devised—a panel interface connector branded GracePort—launched the business, which later became Grace Engineered Products and, in 2020, Grace Technologies.

Today Grace manufactures three core product lines: GracePort panel interface connectors that provide thru-door access to PLCs and control panels; GracePESDs, permanent electrical safety devices that combine presence-of-voltage LED indication with absence-of-voltage test points for lockout/tagout; and the GraceSense predictive maintenance system, a line of IIoT smart sensors that continuously monitor vibration and temperature on switchgear, transformers, and rotating equipment. The catalog encompasses roughly 16,000 SKUs, and the company ships orders worldwide within two to three business days through a distribution network spanning more than 60 countries.

Customers include power plants, food and beverage facilities, chiller plants, and any operation that relies on evaporative cooling towers, as well as broader industrial facilities seeking to reduce incident energy exposure and unplanned downtime. The company’s predictive maintenance webinar series, co-hosted with Infinite Cooling, targets managers of those specific asset types.

Grace says its differentiation rests on four pillars: thought leadership from a team of forward‑thinking engineers; mass customization enabled by its deep SKU count; fast delivery via the 2‑3‑day global shipping promise; and a founding principle of being easy to do business with. Its stated mission is to pursue practical ways of keeping live voltage away from workers before, during, and after maintenance tasks, while empowering maintenance programs with IIoT sensor data.
