## About
Alaska Commercial Electronics (ACE) has operated from Petersburg, Alaska since its founding in 1986 by Jerry Whitethorn and Richard Tamblin. The partners built a dedicated facility in 1994 that remains the company's base. In 2017, ownership transferred to Eric and Adele Rosvold as the founders transitioned to retirement, with technicians Megan O'Neil and Cynthia Benitz joining to continue the service legacy.

ACE sells, services, and installs marine electronics from brands including Nobeltec/TimeZero, Furuno, Icom, Morad, Simrad, and Comnav. The company also provides marine electrical services such as AC/DC installation, panel replacement, and vessel alarm systems. Historically, ACE designed industrial control systems for water treatment, power, and sewer treatment plants, built a solar-powered mountain-top radio repeater for wireless remote control of a hydro-electric plant, and in 1994 produced the Watch Clock, a microprocessor-based vessel watch alarm still in service decades later.

The primary customer base is Southeast Alaska's commercial fishing fleet, which operates from California to the Aleutian Islands. ACE also serves onshore industrial clients, including municipal water and wastewater utilities, power generation facilities, and remote telecommunications sites.

The company emphasizes that reliable equipment and timely repair are critical during short fishing openings and hostile conditions. Its guiding principles are captured in the statements "Serving our customers is our number one priority" and "We service what we sell," with professional installation offered in Alaska or anywhere on the West Coast.

# Albuquerque Fashion Incubator

**Old description:** The Albuquerque Fashion Incubator offers a one-of-a-kind, hands-on training opportunity in sewn product manufacturing. It is the only one of its kind in the world. Really.

## About
Albuquerque Fashion Incubator (ABQFI) operates from Albuquerque, New Mexico, where it has hosted apparel manufacturing boot camps since 2015. The program was founded by Kathleen Fasanella, principal of Apparel Technical Services and the Albuquerque Sewing Factory, who brings nearly four decades of production experience. Organized as a 501(c)3 non-profit, ABQFI began with a single “Coats for Kids” event and now runs two annual cycles of pre-production and production camps at the Albuquerque Sewing Factory on Old Coors Drive.

The incubator’s core activity is a four-day, hands-on boot camp that takes participants through the full sewn-product manufacturing process: pattern creation, production pattern auditing, work-instruction development, fabric spreading, cutting, sewing, and finishing. Each cycle includes a pre-production camp roughly three months before the production camp; attendees who register for pre-production are automatically enrolled in the production phase. Camps are capped at 25 participants, and the inaugural event produced 125 fully lined winter coats in four days using industrial equipment at the factory.

The training targets entrepreneurs, aspiring designers, and individuals with no prior factory experience—75 percent of the first cohort had never used an industrial machine. Finished garments are donated to qualified New Mexico charities serving children, seniors, and other underserved groups. The program also offers custom boot camps for larger groups by arrangement.

Fasanella launched the incubator to address the loss of domestic production knowledge, arguing that re-shoring requires people who know how to run factories. The model combines free instruction (participants cover only material costs) with charitable output, positioning ABQFI as a unique workforce-development and community-service hybrid.
