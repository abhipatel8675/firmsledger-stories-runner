## About
Amish Heirlooms Furniture was founded in 2003 by Travis and Jackie Olson in Mason City, Iowa. The family-owned business began after the couple encountered Amish craftsmanship on fishing trips to southeastern Minnesota, inspiring them to open a store dedicated to handcrafted solid-wood furniture. The company has since relocated and expanded four times, with its current showroom located in Clear Lake, Iowa.

The retailer specializes in made-to-order Amish furniture built by individual Amish families in Indiana. Each piece is constructed from solid wood and can be customized to fit a customer’s style and space. Product lines span bedroom, living room, dining room, office, and outdoor furniture, as well as home decor. In addition, the company operates America’s Mattress at the same location, offering pocketed-coil, memory-foam, and latex mattresses with cooling and pressure-relief technologies.

Amish Heirlooms serves residential customers primarily across Iowa, Minnesota, and the broader Midwest. Its showroom in Clear Lake welcomes walk-in shoppers, while the made-to-order model allows families to specify dimensions, wood species, and finishes for pieces such as dining tables, bedroom sets, and outdoor chairs.

The Olsons founded the business on a respect for traditional joinery and a desire to bring heirloom-quality furniture to the region. The company backs its outdoor line with a 100-year promise, emphasizing durability and generational longevity as core differentiators.

## About
Andersen Self-Service Auto Parts operates from Kearney, Nebraska, where it has been based since 1960. The business traces its roots to 1937, when Ray Andersen founded Andersen Wrecking in downtown Gibbon, Nebraska. After relocating to Kearney, the company expanded into metals and vehicle purchasing, later acquiring Stan’s Auto Parts in 1989 to launch Andersen Auto Parts, a discount outlet for classic and older-model vehicle components.

In 2009, the company transformed its property into a self-service facility, crushing roughly 1,400 vehicles to make room for an inventory that now exceeds 1,700 cars, trucks, campers, and motorcycles. Customers bring their own tools and remove the parts they need, a model that allows the company to offer prices up to 80 percent below conventional auto-parts retailers. The yard is organized by vehicle make and model, and staff provide parts-interchange assistance to ensure the correct component is located. The inventory spans vehicles from the 1940s through the mid-2000s.

The operation serves retail customers, classic-car restorers, motorcycle enthusiasts, and independent repair shops seeking affordable used components. While the primary clientele is individual vehicle owners, the breadth of inventory also supports professional mechanics looking for discontinued or hard-to-find parts.

The shift to self-service was driven by a desire to pass maximum savings to customers; by eliminating the labor cost of part removal, the company can maintain low prices while keeping the facility clean, organized, and safe.
