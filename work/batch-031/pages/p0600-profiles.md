## About
OVR Technology is based in Burlington, Vermont, where a multidisciplinary team of industrial designers, fragrance designers, neuroscientists, and software engineers developed the Omara Scent Display, a personal device that brings olfactory immersion to video games.

The company produces two models: the Omara Core, a desktop scent display with ten scent channels priced at $179, and the Omara Pro, a portable advanced display with sixteen channels priced at $649. Both units combine hardware, proprietary scent cartridges or cassettes, and software to deliver scent in burst or continuous modes, with a time-to-perception ranging from 500 to 1,000 milliseconds.

OVR Technology serves the video gaming and virtual reality markets, targeting both end users—gamers seeking deeper immersion—and creators, including independent game studios worldwide that partner with the company to integrate scent into their titles.

The venture was driven by the ambition to add a missing sensory layer to digital experiences, summarized by the company's statement that "gaming has never felt more alive" and its willingness to tackle the difficulty of being first in digital scent technology.

## About
OXOS Medical, Inc. was founded in June 2016 in Atlanta, Georgia, as Micro C, LLC, before adopting its current name. The company was started by Evan M. Ruff, a computer engineer and MBA from Georgia Tech, and Dr. Gregory P. Kolovich, a reconstructive hand surgeon trained at Harvard Medical School. Kolovich's frustration with the traditional imaging process during surgery sparked the idea for a portable dynamic digital radiography device.

The company develops and manufactures portable X‑ray systems, including the Micro C and the newer MC2, both cleared by the U.S. FDA under 510(k). The devices are designed for low radiation exposure, modular portability, and a no‑fire positioning system that optimizes alignment. A proprietary software module called AiLARA automates dose optimization per the ALARA principle. The team comprises engineers, designers, and clinical advisors, though headcount and facility size are not disclosed.

OXOS serves orthopedics, pediatrics, and sports medicine, selling directly to clinics, hospitals, and surgical practices. Testimonials cite use by hand and upper‑extremity surgeons, foot‑and‑ankle specialists, and team physicians for professional sports organizations such as the Jacksonville Jaguars. The systems are marketed for point‑of‑need imaging in diverse care settings.

The founders state the mission is to let anyone, anywhere access radiographic diagnostics at the point of need. They emphasize three pillars: safety through low‑dose profiles, simplicity via portable and modular design, and intelligence through real‑time alignment and automated dose control.
