## About
Plasman's Lawrenceburg, Tennessee facility operates as part of a global automotive components manufacturer headquartered in Windsor, Canada, with European headquarters in Gothenburg, Sweden. The company traces its roots back over 45 years to a two-person tool shop that grew into a multinational operation spanning nine countries across North America, Europe, and Asia.

The Lawrenceburg site contributes to Plasman's single-source capability covering tooling, injection molding, chroming, paint applications, exterior trim, and assembly. The company runs over 290,000 square feet of dedicated tooling space and employs more than 200 engineers and technicians focused on molding. Its exterior trim sector produces window assemblies, regulators, rack lifts, door frames, and roof rail systems, while additional lines deliver bumpers, fascias, grilles, and subsystem components. Assembly relies on fully automated, complex robotic systems, and surface finishing includes the proprietary Omniluxe® PVD coating and Brilliance paint-over-chrome processes.

Plasman serves automotive OEMs worldwide, providing components that integrate into vehicle exteriors. Its global footprint — with manufacturing, engineering, and sales locations in Canada, the United States, Mexico, Sweden, Norway, Belgium, Czech Republic, Germany, and Japan — allows it to support customers across major automotive markets.

The company emphasizes a collaborative mindset backed by personal commitment and attention to detail, aiming to deliver the right part at the right time. Innovations such as Omniluxe®, an environmentally friendly alternative to traditional chrome plating, and the Brilliance finish reflect its focus on meeting evolving OEM demands and environmental regulations.

They purchased a used thermoforming machine at auction, installed it in a machine shed, and began producing thermoformed parts.

Today the manufacturer provides custom thermoforming, composites, and urethane molding, supported by in-house design and tooling, CAD services, and robotic CNC trimming. The facility houses multiple thermoforming machines capable of handling both small and large parts, and the company's proprietary TEC® (Tooless Engineered Composites) process combines plastics and composites to produce Class-A surfaces with reduced tooling. Secondary operations and assembly are also offered.

The company serves agriculture, construction, dunnage, material handling, medical, OEM, outdoor/recreation, aerospace, industrial, and transportation markets. Its agricultural roots give it particular insight into farm equipment components, while its composites and urethane capabilities address medical device housings, construction equipment hoods, and returnable packaging for industrial clients.

The founders chose thermoforming for its lower entry cost compared to injection molding and built the business on farming values—planet stewardship, quality, innovation, a can-do attitude, and customer service.
