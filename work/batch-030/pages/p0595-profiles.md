## About
Nebraska Public Power District (NPPD) is a publicly-owned utility headquartered in Columbus, Nebraska. It was formed on January 1, 1970, through the merger of Consumers Public Power District, Platte Valley Public Power and Irrigation District, and Nebraska Public Power System. As a political subdivision of the State of Nebraska, NPPD is governed by an 11-member Board of Directors elected from its chartered territory, which spans all or parts of 84 of the state's 93 counties.

NPPD generates, transmits, and distributes electric power across its service territory. Its generation portfolio includes wind, coal, hydroelectric, nuclear, and natural gas facilities, providing a diverse mix of baseload and renewable energy. The utility operates customer service centers throughout the state, offering call answering, dispatch, and start/stop services for both NPPD and partner utilities. It serves more than 95,000 retail customers directly and provides wholesale power to 35 municipalities and 23 public power districts or cooperatives.

The district's customers include residential, commercial, agricultural, and industrial users across 81 Nebraska communities. Wholesale partners include municipal utilities such as those in Norfolk and other cities, as well as rural electric cooperatives. NPPD also administers energy efficiency programs like EnergyWise, which offers incentives for smart thermostats and lawn equipment.

NPPD's founding merger was driven by a desire to consolidate public power resources and ensure reliable, affordable electricity for Nebraskans. The utility emphasizes its public-power model—local control, not-for-profit operation, and a commitment to community investment—as its defining difference from investor-owned utilities.
